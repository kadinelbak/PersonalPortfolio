## Retractor Arm

Mechanical design concept for a low-profile surgical retractor support arm intended to improve positioning stability while preserving access around the patient.

<img src="../../assets/img/retractor-arm/locking-mechanism.png" alt="CAD render of a mechanical locking mechanism for the retractor arm" />

## Clinical Design Context

Open surgical exposure requires reliable retraction while avoiding unnecessary clutter around the operative field. The concept was framed around the need for a compact arm that can position a retractor, maintain clearance above the patient, and resist slip under applied torque.

<img src="../../assets/img/retractor-arm/patient-clearance.png" alt="Concept sketch showing the retractor arm positioned above the patient with clearance" />

## Core Design Question

The central engineering question was how to lock an adjustable arm securely without relying only on friction. A friction-based lock can slip if clamp force drops or surface conditions change. A mechanical lock transfers load through geometry, reducing dependence on clamping force.

<img src="../../assets/img/retractor-arm/lock-comparison.png" alt="Comparison between friction-based locking and mechanical locking for torque load transfer" />

## Design Direction

- Used mechanical engagement to carry load through interlocking geometry.
- Explored spline-like features and a locking knob to resist rotation.
- Considered arm clearance, retractor positioning, and fast adjustment during use.
- Balanced stability against device profile so the design would not obstruct the working area.

## Technical Contribution

The work combined clinical problem framing, mechanism selection, CAD iteration, and mechanical reasoning around torque, load transfer, and slip risk. The final portfolio visuals were extracted from the project presentation deck and emphasize the public-facing design logic rather than graphic operative imagery.

## Next Iteration

Future work would focus on quantifying torque resistance, testing lock repeatability, refining sterilizable geometry, and validating whether the mechanism can be adjusted quickly while maintaining stable retraction.

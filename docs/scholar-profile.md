    <figure>
      <img src="{{ '/assets/img/research-showcase/research_stage_flow.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Research workflow diagram showing stages of disease and treatment modeling" />

    <div>
      <h3>Research Focus</h3>
      <p>This project focuses on mathematical modeling of ovarian cancer population dynamics, with an emphasis on interpretable growth behavior, treatment response, and adaptive-therapy framing. The work turns experimental cell-growth measurements into parameterized ordinary differential equation models that can be compared across monoculture and coculture conditions.</p>
      <h3>Project Responsibilities</h3>
      <p>My responsibilities center on building and organizing the fitting workflow, comparing model forms, exporting fitted parameters, and interpreting how population behavior changes across experimental settings. The work connects experimental cancer biology to computational modeling by turning raw growth-curve measurements into reproducible analysis outputs.</p>
      <p><a class="document-download" href="{{ '/projects/disease-treatment-modeling/' | relative_url }}">Open full research project</a></p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="scholar-feature scholar-feature--reverse">

      <figcaption>Representative QuPath analysis state used to support scalable image-derived cell counting.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <div>
      <h3>Research Infrastructure</h3>
      <p>In a related BEAT Cancer Lab project, I built and refined a QuPath cell-counting workflow for batch image ingestion, detection setup, quality-control review, and CSV export. This work helped reduce repetitive manual counting and made large image datasets easier to move into downstream analysis.</p>
